Welcome! I am a **statistician** and **Research Fellow** at [Dana-Farber Cancer Institute](https://www.dana-farber.org/) and an affiliate of the Department of Biostatistics at the [Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health](https://hsph.harvard.edu) in Boston, Massachusetts, USA. I develop Bayesian statistical and machine learning methods for high-dimensional biomedical data. My current research focuses on Bayesian discrete matrix factorization, interpretable probabilistic modeling, and statistical learning, with applications to cancer genomics and precision medicine.

More broadly, I am interested in developing Bayesian methods for unsupervised learning, latent variable modeling, and uncertainty quantification that enable interpretable analysis of complex, high-dimensional data. My work combines statistical theory, computational methodology, and scientific applications in genomics and biomedical research.

## Research Interests

- Bayesian statistics and probabilistic machine learning
- Unsupervised learning and latent variable models
- High-dimensional statistical inference
- Cancer genomics, including copy-number alteration, RNA-seq, and microarray data
- Reproducible research, and statistical software development in R and Python

I earned a Ph.D. in Probability and Statistics from [CIMAT, A.C.](https://www.cimat.mx/) under the supervision of **Graciela González-Farías** and **Terry Speed**. My doctoral research focused on statistical genomics, including classification methods for microarray gene expression data and statistical methods for studying gene interaction networks in health and disease.

Before joining Dana-Farber and Harvard, I was a **Senior Lecturer** in Kenya, where I taught undergraduate and graduate statistics and conducted research in financial time series, exchange-rate volatility, macroeconomic forecasting, and statistical learning for biological data. I also held a **Visiting Research Fellowship** at the Walter and Eliza Hall Institute of Medical Research in Melbourne, Australia.

I am passionate about developing rigorous and interpretable statistical methods that bridge Bayesian statistics, machine learning, and data science to advance our understanding of cancer and other complex biological systems.
